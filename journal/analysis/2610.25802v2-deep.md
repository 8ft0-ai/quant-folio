## Executive research judgement

**[ANALYSIS]** The contribution worth investigating is not a higher-return strategy but a disciplined separation between *what a spread seems to be doing* and *how certain an estimator should be about that judgement*. The Neural Filter Estimator (NFE) amortises posterior inference across simulated mean-reverting spreads and gives each real spread a distribution over reversion half-life. This enables an uncertainty penalty in position sizing and a probabilistic assessment of convergence. It is a credible research design for uncertainty-aware signal governance, but the paper itself offers strong reasons **not** to promote it as a trading-alpha discovery. [Abstract and §1, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=1).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** With the same Attention Factor Model (AFM) residuals, the later-period Sharpe ratio is 1.45 for the base rule and 1.49 for NFE posterior sizing. The author explicitly calls this difference unresolved relative to the reported standard error. Predictive distributions do not beat an unconditional benchmark on proper scoring metrics, and simple global shrinkage can reproduce most of the forecast–realised gap reduction. These are cautionary **negative or near-null results**, not evidence of transferable profit. [Table 1, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=5); [Table 2 and §4.2, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=7).

**[ANALYSIS]** Verdict: **RESEARCH-WORTHY / NO DEPLOYMENT CLAIM**. The strongest transferable idea is a falsifiable calibration and decision-surprise benchmark. The most important threats are an incompletely tested simulator, posterior coverage demonstrated mainly on the training prior, use of a full-sample two-sample diagnostic, look-ahead in the stock universe, and incomplete implementation costs. These restrict the decision to a separate, read-only validation study.

## 1. What the model actually estimates

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** Section 3.1 starts with daily asset returns and characteristics, constructs factor residual returns and cumulatively sums those residuals into a spread. The filter reads a look-back of **L = 60 trading days** and the portfolio holds a position for **H = 20 trading days** in the empirical experiment. The two stages have distinct purposes: the cross-sectional factor model removes common returns; the time-series filter infers a hidden property of the residual spread. This is not a neural network forecasting raw share prices. [§3.1 and Figure 1, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=3).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** Equation (1) assumes the spread's next increment follows a state-dependent conditional law. Definition 3.1 and Equation (2) define regimes by the expected fraction of a deviation reversed within the holding horizon, rather than by an ex-post return label. Equation (3) instantiates a regime-indexed Ornstein–Uhlenbeck-type reversion kernel: change in spread equals a speed times distance from a latent level, plus a volatility-scaled innovation. For constant speed, half-life is **tau = ln(2)/kappa**. A threshold of **tau = 60 days** divides a faster **C** state from a slower **D** state; over 20 days this corresponds to about 21% expected reversion at the threshold. These are modelling definitions, not observed market regimes. [§3.2, Definition 3.1 and Equations (1)–(3), p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=3).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** The speed, level and innovation scale are partly latent. Appendix A specifies autoregressive log-volatility, a random-walking level and centred asymmetric lognormal innovations; the regime index follows a first-order Markov transition matrix. The two-state process and the training process are not identical in every detail: network training holds the half-life fixed over a window and samples it from a broad log-uniform prior. This simplification is essential when interpreting posterior calibration on simulator draws. [§3.5, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=5); [Appendix A, Equations (8), Tables 3–4, pp. 8–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** In Equation (5), factor weights and loadings used for day t are dated t−1, an explicit causal convention. The empirical NFE consumes AFM residuals, rather than jointly learning replacement factors. A gated recurrent unit (GRU) encodes each normalised historical spread window. A five-component mixture-density head outputs a density over **log2 half-life**, and Equation (6) integrates the mass above the half-life threshold to estimate the probability of the slow regime. Figure 1 separates residual construction, the GRU and mixture head, the synthetic-prior training path and the fixed decision layer. [§3.3, Equations (5)–(6) and Figure 1, pp. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=4).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** Equation (7) takes posterior expectations and a variance term to size a contrarian position. When half-life is uncertain, the additional variance penalises exposure to poorly resolved reversions; it is zero with a point estimate. The rule subsequently demeans exposures and scales to unit gross, so uncertainty largely redistributes *relative* allocations rather than automatically reducing overall gross exposure. Critically, the variance penalty models uncertainty in **reversion speed**, not uncertainty in the unknown **spread level**. The author identifies level uncertainty as the dominant identification limitation. The reported regime probability is not itself the weight-setting input. [§3.4 and Equation (7), pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=4).

**[ANALYSIS]** That distinction matters in implementation. Calling this a generic Bayesian risk-aware trading policy would overstate it: the inference is amortised under a particular synthetic prior, the posterior is subsequently reweighted with stock-specific empirical-Bayes priors, the mean/variance formula is tied to an assumed OU kernel, and fixed gross exposure can mask absolute uncertainty. An independent experiment must freeze residuals, windows, exposures and costs before attributing any difference to posterior estimation.

## 2. Simulator fit, training and calibration

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** The author uses a classifier two-sample test to discriminate real from simulated residual windows. Table 3 shows Gaussian constant-volatility windows at **0.903/0.904 AUC** for crypto/KRX and the selected stochastic-volatility-plus-asymmetric-lognormal law at **0.686/0.678**. The acceptance rule is AUC plus two standard errors below **0.75**. The selected process passes this convention, but remains distinguishable from real data. Positive and null controls are reported; changing the reversion kernel barely moves the classifier AUC, so the test primarily assesses the innovation law, not the mean-reversion dynamics. [Appendix A, Table 3, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[ANALYSIS]** A process being *harder to distinguish* is not proof that its state posterior is calibrated under the real data-generating process. The test uses scale-invariant window statistics, and Appendix A states that the real-window diagnostic was formed from the **full sample**. This is a research-selection/look-ahead concern even though the paper says network weights train only on simulated windows and the moment matching uses training splits. It should be treated as a source-design weakness rather than a demonstrated trading-data leak. A genuinely chronological version must repeat model-law selection within training history only. [§3.5, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=5); [Appendix A, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** Table 5 specifies a GRU of width 64, five-component mixture head, **60,000** synthetic draws, 4,000 Adam steps with batch 512, and a log-uniform half-life prior from **3 to 400 days**. The paper describes fitting stock-specific priors from training-split half-life blocks, and applying them through posterior reweighting without retraining the GRU. The same table gives a separate panel-network experimental configuration. These are author-documented settings, not settings verified against a local run. [§§3.4–3.5, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=5); [Table 5, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** Figure 2(b) plots nominal versus empirical credible-interval coverage for the half-life on simulated truth-known draws. The maximum absolute coverage deviation is reported at **0.022** in the illustrated seed and at most **0.010** in two further seeds; deliberately halving posterior width yields undercoverage of **0.08–0.12**. The check is conducted under the training prior **before** per-asset reweighting, and is not an independent real-market half-life truth test. The author points out that a posterior ignoring the input window could also pass a marginal coverage test. [§4.2 and Figure 2(b), p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=6).

**[ANALYSIS]** Marginal interval coverage cannot by itself validate conditional calibration near the threshold where position choice changes. The minimum independent package would test rank/PIT behaviour, credible-interval coverage *and width*, posterior concentration and conditional reliability across speed, level drift, volatility, spread magnitude and regime transitions. Priors after stock-specific reweighting require a separate calibration check; otherwise the headline coverage result cannot simply be carried through to the deployed decision.

## 3. Data, fair comparators and author-reported outcomes

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** The empirical panel comprises **120 KRX stocks** with complete histories selected from a top-200 liquidity screen, covering **2010–2026** (4,072 trading days). Table 5 describes a 2018-04 split, first 30% of each period used for training, annual AFM refits, rolling PCA with three components over 252 days, 20-day non-overlapping holdings, 5 basis points per turnover unit and a 1 basis point daily borrow charge on short notional. Firm fundamentals are documented as point-in-time from 2016; public adjusted daily data, with an external code/data-pipeline repository, are cited by the author. [§4 and Table 5, pp. 5 and 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** Table 1 reports later-period **net-of-modelled-cost** Sharpe ratios: **FF3 −0.12**, **PCA 0.57**, **AFM 1.45**, **NFE 1.49**, with a random benchmark at **−0.23**. AFM and NFE have equal reported turnover (**0.055**); their reported maximum drawdowns are **4.2%** and **4.1%** respectively. The reported standard error of strategy-versus-random differential Sharpe is approximately **0.41**, much larger than the AFM–NFE absolute Sharpe difference of 0.04. The author says no method outperforms the benchmark in the earlier period. Consequently neither NFE superiority nor unconditional strategy persistence is established. [Table 1 and §4.1, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=5).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** Truth-known tests in §4.2 put practical regime detectors, including the amortised posterior and point estimate, at **0.61–0.66 AUC**; a detector given the true level and scale reaches **0.93**. Supplying the level boosts separation from roughly **0.62–0.64** to **0.79–0.81**, identifying level estimation as a bottleneck. A 25 × 256 particle filter has reported half-life rank correlation **0.74** versus **0.59** for NFE, but costs **7.9 seconds** versus **2.5 milliseconds per cross-section** on the author's desktop CPU. These are reported within that benchmark, not independently timed or guaranteed on another machine. [§4.2, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=6).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** Table 2 is decisive for the interpretation. Against the unconditional predictive baseline, continuous ranked probability skill scores are **−0.97** for the OU point estimate, **−0.50** for a mixture-density network, **−0.11** for amortised OU-form NFE, **0.00** for the direct amortised variant and **−0.03** for boosted quantiles. The nominal 90% coverage is about **0.89** for both amortised variants, but conformal calibration is applied across learned methods. Forecast–realised return gaps are **1,084 basis points per hold** for the OU point estimator, **588** for OU-form NFE and **137** for the direct variant, at unit gross. Gap reduction must not be equated with return improvement: a forecast of zero mechanically has a small gap. [Table 2 and §4.2, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=7).

**[AUTHOR-REPORTED]** A point forecast multiplied by one scalar chosen on the training split can match the OU-form posterior's gap within sampling uncertainty. The manuscript confirms several findings using disjoint seeds or periods, explicitly withdraws a claimed 250-day window advantage as unstable, and identifies an **overshoot** effect that reverses the trading interpretation of its regime labels. [§§4.1–4.2 and §5, pp. 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=7).

**[ANALYSIS]** The informative comparison is **posterior versus globally shrunk point forecast**, on identical AFM residuals, as well as against a simpler probabilistic state filter. Comparing NFE's 1.49 Sharpe against a PCA baseline at 0.57 confounds improved factor construction with posterior value. An apparent improvement in calibration must survive null predictors and conditional tests, not just attractive cumulative-return plots.

## 4. Evidence quality, failure modes and reproducibility

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** Appendix B explicitly acknowledges **end-of-sample liquidity ranking and exclusion of delisted stocks**. This means the retrospective stock set benefits from future knowledge. It also states that implied factor-hedge legs are not separately traded or costed, and that Korean transaction tax and short-sale restrictions during part of the sample are not modelled. The author cautions that reported returns are **not implementable returns**. These statements materially weaken economic extrapolation while increasing the transparency of the paper's limitations. [Appendix B, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[ANALYSIS]** Investigate four distinct risks. **(1) Universe look-ahead:** reconstruct monthly membership using information available on each date, retaining delisted names. **(2) Feature and residual timing:** independently audit corporate-disclosure lags, adjusted prices, the t−1 factor-weight convention, training-only priors and all annual refits. **(3) Evaluation contamination:** examine window overlaps, 20-day return labelling and the exact purge/embargo needed at every split. **(4) Cost omissions:** model hedge-leg execution, taxes, borrow availability, recalls, capacity and slippage, including shorting restrictions. These are validation priorities, not accusations that all controls failed. [§3.3, Equation (5), p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=4); [Appendix B, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9).

**[ANALYSIS]** The simulator remains a structural risk because the two-sample test does not validate the latent-level process or the OU kernel, and passing AUC 0.75 tolerates meaningful separability. The half-life mixture can be well calibrated for its own simulated world while misrepresenting structural breaks, jumps, cross-sectional dependence or non-stationary trading constraints. The paper's positive identification-control result suggests a potentially larger payoff from better level information or a different observation model than from a more sophisticated posterior head alone. [Appendix A, Tables 3–4, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=9); [§4.2, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=6).

**[SOURCE-CHECKED]** The manuscript points to [the author's code repository](https://github.com/minkeymouse/nfe). This assessment has not audited that repository's current contents, run its scripts, retrieved underlying market data or regenerated tables. The PDF and its precise SHA are verified; code-to-paper equivalence, data licences, model seeds, claimed confirmation runs and economic sensitivity remain unverified. ## 5. Falsifiable, read-only validation protocol

**[PROPOSED / NOT RUN]** The following is an **evaluation design**, not a backtest, approval to acquire data or a live trading recommendation.

**Gate A — freeze source and causal data contract.** Record arXiv version and the repository commit separately. Obtain licensed or publicly usable point-in-time prices, corporate actions, fundamentals, borrow restrictions and universe membership. Time-stamp when each observation became available. Reproduce the author's 60-day look-back, 20-day holding horizon, costs and gross constraints, while separately building a stricter investable-universe scenario. Version every data slice and transformation. **Stop** if delisting survivorship, unavailable shorting or factor-construction look-ahead cannot be bounded.

**Gate B — pre-register controlled baselines.** Keep the same time-stamped residuals and signals for: (i) OU point-estimate and original contrarian sizing; (ii) the same point forecast with a single scalar calibrated only on training data; (iii) a simple probabilistic filter, such as a correctly specified linear-Gaussian state-space/Kalman variant on its valid assumptions, or a small particle/HMM filter when regimes make Gaussian assumptions untenable; (iv) NFE with and without posterior sizing; and (v) unconditional and random-walk negative controls. Compare PCA and AFM residual constructions **within** these matched estimators, rather than conflating factor gains with inference gains. Track compute and parameter count.

**Gate C — chronological evidence integrity.** Use expanding, walk-forward training and disjoint future evaluation windows with contiguous date boundaries. Fit the simulator family, two-sample law selection, hyper-parameters, stock-specific priors, model selection and conformal calibration **only inside the historical training/calibration fold**. Separate a calibration tail and enforce an embargo at least as large as the maximum future outcome horizon (20 trading days), augmented where features require it. For long rolling windows, audit all 60-day input dependencies rather than assuming a 20-day purge cures everything. Use one terminal untouched period for acceptance; keep earlier windows exploratory. Block use of future-survivor lists.

**Gate D — simulator and inference validity.** On fresh truth-known draws from both admitted and deliberately misspecified simulators, measure regime AUC, half-life rank correlation, interval coverage and width at 50%, 80%, 90% and 95%, PIT or simulation-based calibration ranks, and proper log/CRPS scores when defined. Stratify by threshold proximity, speed, volatility, jumps, shifting levels, transition frequency and asset liquidity. Run an input-ignorant prior as a calibration negative control and a true-level oracle as positive control. Repeat after per-stock prior reweighting; test whether nominal coverage remains credible **conditional** on the input, not merely marginally. **Falsify** the claimed reliability if apparent coverage is due to prior-only predictions or fails materially under modest misspecification.

**Gate E — predictive quality and decision surprise.** For held-out 20-day spread changes, calculate CRPS, log score where support permits, 90% interval coverage/width, empirical PIT, conditional mean bias and return-forecast versus realised gap, with uncertainty intervals computed by date-block/bootstrap clustering. Require a material improvement **over both unconditional forecasts and the scalar-shrunk point control** at matched dispersion, not just a smaller raw gap. Test whether posteriority contributes value beyond the d-squared weighting effect by fixing its width across windows, then varying only conditional width. **Falsify** an adaptive-calibration claim if global shrinkage matches outcomes within pre-registered uncertainty.

**Gate F — economic and operational reality.** Only after statistical gates pass, evaluate cost-aware hypothetical net outcomes under matched gross/net, turnover, holding times and rebalancing frequency; show gross and cost components separately. Include realistic hedge-leg execution, borrow constraints, short bans, taxes, slippage, market impact and stress periods. Report Sharpe differences **with confidence intervals**, drawdown, tail exposures, cross-sectional concentration and capacity. Test across disjoint dates, seeds and one independent market without treating an uncalibrated transfer as validation. Require a positive, economically material net difference after complete costs and robust uncertainty; otherwise retain NFE solely as a diagnostic layer. No capital allocation follows from this protocol.

**Decision rule.** Before running anything, the owner must set numerical acceptance tolerances for calibration error, proper-score difference, confidence bands, implementation-cost coverage and minimum economic significance, using a small development fold and then freezing them. The primary claim tested is **reliable conditional uncertainty at fixed information**, not profitable statistical arbitrage. Passing simulation-only calibration is insufficient for deployment; failure of the source-integrity or chronological gate blocks every later claim.

## 6. Open questions and final verdict

**[ANALYSIS]** Can the posterior remain calibrated after the stock-specific prior is applied and the market regime changes? Does its width improve decisions beyond a simple scaled forecast and the d-squared weighting term? Can a causally estimated level — perhaps via a joint cross-sectional state-space model — recover more of the large identified information gap? Does the simulator remain plausible for delistings, stressed liquidity and changing shorting conditions? Would any advantage survive executable hedge-leg costs in another market? These remain questions, not paper-proven opportunities. [§4.2 and §5, pp. 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25802v2#page=8).

**Verdict — INVESTIGATE METHODS, NOT RETURNS.** **[ANALYSIS]** This is an unusually useful paper because it measures failure modes, reports strong negative comparisons and distinguishes posterior credibility from actual trading outcomes. The best next research investment is a bounded, leakage-controlled replication of the **calibration and decision-surprise** claims against cheap controls, while retaining the baseline journal snapshot as abstract-level context. Do **not** infer that an apparent 1.49 Sharpe is causal, executable or portable. No validation or trading is authorised by this assessment.
