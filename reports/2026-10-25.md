## 2. Research cards

### 1. [Probabilistic Limit Order Book Forecasting with Conditional Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25495)

**Theme:** Market microstructure · **arXiv:** 2610.25495 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-18

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Introduces LOBGrad, a conditional diffusion model with RNN encoder for probabilistic limit order book forecasting, establishing an excess-risk oracle inequality for the noise-prediction objective and demonstrating improved queue metrics and calibrated intervals on crypto and equity data.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Provides both theoretical guarantees (oracle inequality under mixing conditions) and strong empirical validation across multiple asset classes, advancing probabilistic LOB forecasting with a principled diffusion framework.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 2. [Understanding the Inner Workings of a State-Space Foundation Model for Limit Order Books](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28753)

**Theme:** Market microstructure · **arXiv:** 2610.28753 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-20

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Analyzes an 8.2M-parameter Mamba-3 state-space model trained on tokenized LOB message streams, finding it learns a general, causally steerable internal representation of market microstructure (order-flow imbalance R²≈0.59) that transfers to unseen stocks.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Offers rare interpretability evidence that autoregressive foundation models acquire transferable world models of market dynamics, crucial for trusting and deploying such models in trading.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 3. [Rachev-Ratio Portfolio Optimization with TimeGAN Scenarios: Replacement versus Supplementation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24544)

**Theme:** Portfolio and risk · **arXiv:** 2610.24544 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-17

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Evaluates TimeGAN-generated scenarios for Rachev-ratio portfolio optimization across 12 ETFs, finding synthetic scenarios understate volatility and tail dependence, with no causal performance improvement over historical resampling despite higher average returns in some hybrid configurations.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Demonstrates financial model risk from imperfect generative scenarios interacting with tail-sensitive allocation, providing a rigorous negative result that cautions against uncritical use of synthetic data in portfolio construction.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 4. [LOB-Impact Benchmark: Emergent Market Impact in Generative Limit Order Book Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27100)

**Theme:** Market microstructure · **arXiv:** 2610.27100 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-19

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Introduces a benchmark (LOB-Impact) to evaluate generative limit order book models by measuring emergent market impact. The authors inject metaorders into five neural LOB generators across five NASDAQ stocks and estimate impact exponents (0.63–1.21), finding that impact emerges naturally from next-token prediction training.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Provides a rigorous, counterfactual framework for testing whether generative market simulators reproduce realistic market impact—a critical requirement for training trading agents in simulation. The benchmark isolates learned response from mechanical book effects and validates against known baselines.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 5. [A game-theoretic analysis of the market microstructure in decentralized exchanges with concentrated liquidity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28863)

**Theme:** Market microstructure · **arXiv:** 2610.28863 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-20

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Models strategic liquidity provision in concentrated-liquidity DEXs as a mean-field game. Derives closed-form solutions for optimal range width, value function, stationary position distribution, and a critical volatility threshold above which provision is unprofitable. Empirical validation on WETH/USDC pools shows the critical volatility tracks realized volatility.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Offers a first-principles, game-theoretic foundation for liquidity provider behavior in DeFi, yielding testable predictions (critical volatility, optimal width) without fitted parameters. The framework explains observed on-chain behavior and can guide protocol design and LP strategy.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 6. [Optimal Trading Filters and the Value of Predictive Signals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24558)

**Theme:** Signals and filtering · **arXiv:** 2610.24558 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-17

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Solves the stationary quadratic trading problem with transient price impact using Wiener–Hopf factorization. The optimal policy decomposes into a conditional forecast and an explicit causal linear filter. Recovers the Gârleanu–Pedersen aim portfolio under exponential resilience and derives fractional trading filters for power-law impact decay, revealing an optimal mean-reversion rate for OU signals.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Advances optimal execution theory by unifying prediction and trading costs in a single analytical framework. The factorization approach handles long-memory impact kernels beyond finite-dimensional LQR, and the result on signal value under power-law decay provides practical guidance for signal horizon selection.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 7. [Amortized Neural Filtering for Calibrated Posteriors in Statistical Arbitrage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25802)

**Theme:** Signals and filtering · **arXiv:** 2610.25802 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-19

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Proposes a Neural Filter Estimator (NFE) that amortizes Bayesian filtering for statistical arbitrage, producing calibrated posteriors over latent spread states in a single forward pass. Empirically, on Korean equities the method halves to octuples the gap between forecast and realized returns of conventional point estimates, and position sizing from the posterior matches the Sharpe of an end-to-end Attention Factor Model without added risk.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Addresses a core gap in stat-arb practice—discarding estimation uncertainty—with a scalable amortized inference technique. Provides both controlled simulation evidence (showing correct uncertainty calibration) and live-market validation (Korean equities, net of costs) demonstrating economically significant improvement over point-estimate benchmarks and parity with a strong learned-factor baseline.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.

### 8. [Dynamic portfolio selection under range value-at-risk with logarithmic returns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26919)

**Theme:** Portfolio and risk · **arXiv:** 2610.26919 · **Submitted:** 2026-10-17

**Authors' reported contribution (abstract-based assessment):** Solves the continuous-time mean–Range Value-at-Risk (RVaR) portfolio problem with logarithmic returns. Reduces the dynamic optimization to a static quantile problem, characterizes the optimal terminal wealth via the convex envelope of an RVaR-specific weighting function, and derives closed-form wealth processes and replicating strategies under constant investment opportunities. Shows RVaR nests VaR and Expected Shortfall as limiting cases.

**Potential application (interpretation; adapted from published “Why read it”):** Delivers a complete theoretical framework for portfolio optimization under a flexible, regulatory-relevant risk measure (RVaR) with log returns. The convex-envelope characterization yields explicit multi-region payoff structures, closed-form solutions, and clear limiting behavior, providing both new theoretical insights and practical replication formulas for risk-aware portfolio construction.

**Open question:** Do the reported conclusions generalise beyond the authors' assumptions, datasets and evaluation setting?

**Assessment depth:** Abstract only.
