### 1. [Latent Continuum of Regimes in Limit Order Book Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05740)

**Market microstructure** · arXiv 2610.05740 · 2026-10-05 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | Analyzes high-frequency limit-order-book data for EURO STOXX 50 futures (987 days) using Riemannian geometry on covariance matrices, finding a latent continuum (effective dimension ~1.4) rather than discrete regimes. A dominant coordinate captures 83–85% of variation and tracks VSTOXX without labels; walk-forward tests show only the coarsest macro tier generalizes out-of-sample. |
| **Why investigate** | Challenges the standard discrete-regime paradigm with a geometrically rigorous, label-free framework and a massive clean dataset. The continuum finding has direct implications for regime-aware execution, risk modeling, and market-state forecasting. |

### 2. [Market Impact Estimation via Causal Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12537)

**Market microstructure** · arXiv 2610.12537 · 2026-10-08 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | Introduces a causal prior-data fitted network (PFN) trained on a synthetic market with known confounding between informed flow and price impact. The PFN removes up to 68% overestimation bias of naive regressors, matches an unbiased oracle under high confounding, and transfers zero-shot to ABIDES simulations and real BTC/ETH/SOL and Nasdaq metaorders. |
| **Why investigate** | Reframes market impact as a causal estimand and provides a practical, transferable simulator that corrects confounding bias—a persistent problem in execution-cost models. The synthetic prior with exact confounding control and zero-shot real-market validation are methodologically novel for quantitative finance. |
| **Open question** | Does simulated market impact remain plausible under different order sizes? |

### 3. [FOMO: FLEX Options and Market Outcomes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13044)

**Other quantitative research** · arXiv 2610.13044 · 2026-10-09 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | Uses a linked dataset of FLEX options, listed-option chains, and equity data (250 days, 41 underlyings) to show that FLEX activity increases listed-option open interest (beta=0.306, p=0.004), clusters in high-IV mid-price stocks, and that IV-realized spreads predict stressed short-straddle returns. Equity return effects are short-lived. |
| **Why investigate** | Provides the first systematic evidence that customized FLEX contracts leave measurable footprints in standard option markets, revealing them as signals of institutional volatility demand rather than directional predictors. The clean difference-in-differences design and linked microstructure data make it a high-quality empirical market-structure study. |

### 4. [SOTA: Stock Options Trading Agents Guided by Option-Implied Return Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10407)

**Other quantitative research** · arXiv 2610.10407 · 2026-10-07 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | SOTA introduces an agentic framework for structured option-strategy selection that abstracts the large option universe into strategy-level decisions, using a post-trained LLM (Qwen3.8-27B) with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. Evaluated on options for nine large-cap U.S. equities and SPY, it achieves an 18.3% total return, Sharpe ratio of 1.60, and maximum drawdown of 8.96% over a six-month out-of-sample period. The paper also documents an asymmetric role of news: it improves teacher trajectories but harms out-of-sample performance when retained during RL. |
| **Why investigate** | Provides a novel, empirically validated agentic system for a complex, high-dimensional option-trading problem, demonstrating strong risk-adjusted returns and highlighting a critical nuance in using news data for RL-based trading agents. |

### 5. [MLP-Initialized Reinforcement Learning for Exit Timing in SPX Short-Straddle Portfolios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13126)

**Portfolio and risk** · arXiv 2610.13126 · 2026-10-09 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | This paper develops a two-stage supervised and reinforcement-learning framework for exit timing in SPX short-straddle portfolios. An MLP trained on ex-post optimal hold/exit labels initializes RL actors, which are then fine-tuned with a risk-aware reward. Out-of-sample evaluation (2024-2026) shows the MLP-initialized Actor-Critic averages 37.54% cumulative return and a Sharpe ratio of 1.705, substantially outperforming the supervised MLP alone (35.01%, 1.651) and RL from scratch (0.18%). |
| **Why investigate** | Demonstrates a practical and effective method for a sparse, path-dependent option-exit problem, showing that oracle-guided supervised learning provides a stable foundation for reward-based policy fine-tuning, with strong out-of-sample performance metrics. |

### 7. [Optimal Investment to Reach a Financial Goal: A Stochastic Control Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10046)

**Other quantitative research** · arXiv 2610.10046 · 2026-10-07 · Abstract only

| Field | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding / method** | Introduces a stochastic control framework for goal-based investing where an investor trades until reaching a financial goal or a deadline, maximizing expected discount at goal-reaching time and expected utility of the funding ratio otherwise. Characterizes the value function as a viscosity solution to the HJB equation, provides a complete characterization for infinite horizon with constant goal, and develops a numerical scheme based on Howard's algorithm. Shows optimal policies can be decreasing in risky asset drift and need not converge to full risk-free investment as volatility diverges. |
| **Why investigate** | Formulates a new class of stochastic control problems relevant to practical goal-based investing, with rigorous analysis (viscosity solutions, Bellman principle) and surprising qualitative insights about optimal policies. The combination of theoretical depth and numerical approach advances the literature on optimal investment with targets and deadlines. |
| **Open question** | Which assumptions most affect the proposed method's applicability? |
