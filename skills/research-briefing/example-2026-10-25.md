## The 60-second briefing

This shortlist is dominated by **market microstructure**, but the highest-value research questions are broader: whether market simulators produce credible *impact*, whether predictive signals quantify *uncertainty*, and whether synthetic scenarios preserve *tail risk*. Three themes link the eight papers:

- **Market microstructure (4):** from diffusion forecasting and interpretable LOB models to simulated impact and DEX liquidity behaviour.
- **Signals and filtering (2):** translating signals into trades and retaining uncertainty in statistical-arbitrage estimates.
- **Portfolio and risk (2):** scrutinising generative allocation scenarios and alternative tail-risk objectives.

**Editorial conclusion:** the common question is not merely whether a model forecasts well, but whether its behaviour remains credible under trading costs, uncertainty and stress. This is an interpretation of the reported abstracts, not a verified empirical trend.

## Three editorial priority reads

*These are editorial choices for evaluation methodology and potential research application, **not** the original shortlist order or a new quality ranking. The source shortlist begins with arXiv 2610.25495, 2610.28753 and 2610.24544.*

### 1. [LOB-Impact Benchmark: Emergent Market Impact in Generative Limit Order Book Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27100)
**Original shortlist position:** 4 · **Market microstructure** · **Abstract only**

**Why read it:** the authors describe injecting metaorders into five neural LOB generators and comparing resulting market-impact patterns. That suggests a practical test of whether a simulator is *behaviourally* useful rather than visually convincing.

**Limit:** the reported impact exponents (0.63–1.21) come from the authors' experimental setting; the source briefing does not independently establish realism across order sizes, securities or regimes.

### 2. [Amortized Neural Filtering for Calibrated Posteriors in Statistical Arbitrage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25802)
**Original shortlist position:** 7 · **Signals and filtering** · **Abstract only**

**Why read it:** posterior estimates could allow an evaluation of trading decisions that accounts for uncertainty, rather than treating a single spread forecast as certain.

**Limit:** the authors' results concern their stated simulations and Korean equity setting. Whether calibration transfers across instruments, regimes or market-data gaps is not established here.

### 3. [Rachev-Ratio Portfolio Optimization with TimeGAN Scenarios: Replacement versus Supplementation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24544)
**Original shortlist position:** 3 · **Portfolio and risk** · **Abstract only**

**Why read it:** the reported negative result is a useful caution: synthetic paths may look useful yet understate volatility and tail dependence, undermining risk-sensitive allocation.

**Limit:** the report covers the authors' comparison using 12 ETFs; the conclusion is not proof that all generative scenarios or tail-risk optimisers fail.

## Cross-paper insight — editorial synthesis

The **LOB-Impact** benchmark asks whether simulated trading produces credible market responses. **Neural filtering** asks whether a forecast retains calibrated uncertainty. **TimeGAN/Rachev optimisation** asks whether synthetic data adequately represents the tail. Together they suggest an **evaluation-first** principle: test behavioural validity, predictive calibration and stress realism separately before turning a model output into a trading decision.

This is a cross-paper hypothesis about research practice, not a result demonstrated jointly by the papers.

## The remaining five papers

- [Probabilistic Limit Order Book Forecasting with Conditional Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25495) — LOBGrad's author-reported interval calibration motivates checking regime-dependent uncertainty; **arXiv 2610.25495**, abstract only.
- [Understanding the Inner Workings of a State-Space Foundation Model for Limit Order Books](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28753) — an 8.2-million-parameter Mamba-3 analysis raises questions about transferability of learned microstructure representations; **2610.28753**, abstract only.
- [A game-theoretic analysis of the market microstructure in decentralized exchanges with concentrated liquidity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28863) — concentrated-liquidity mean-field modelling offers a possible framework for testing volatility-sensitive liquidity provision; **2610.28863**, abstract only.
- [Optimal Trading Filters and the Value of Predictive Signals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24558) — combines prediction with transient price impact, making the assumed impact-decay kernel an important modelling risk; **2610.24558**, abstract only.
- [Dynamic portfolio selection under range value-at-risk with logarithmic returns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26919) — theoretical RVaR portfolio controls warrant checking robustness when constant investment assumptions no longer hold; **2610.26919**, abstract only.
